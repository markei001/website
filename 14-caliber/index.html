N° 01 · Mainplate

The floor everything agrees on

A disc of brass, 0.9 mm thick, bored for twenty-one pivots to within two microns. Every other layer is only as accurate as the holes in this one. The grain is perlage: overlapping circles cut by a spinning peg, each a small record of where the tool had been.

Ø 31.0 mm · 21 bores · perlage, rhodium-free
N° 02 · Going train

Four meshes between a spring and a heartbeat

The barrel turns once in eight hours. Pinions of 8, 8, 8 and 6 leaves multiply that: once an hour at the centre, once a minute at the fourth, ten times a minute at the escape wheel. 8 × 8 × 7.5 × 10 is 4,800 to one.

64:8 · 64:8 · 60:8 · 60:6 · module 2.8
N° 03 · Escapement

The part that says no

Left alone the train would unwind in seconds. A 24-tooth wheel is held by two sapphire pallets. Each time the balance sweeps through centre the fork flips, one tooth slips free, and the wheel advances half a tooth, 7.5 degrees, before it is stopped again. Eight times a second.

24 teeth · 7.5° per beat · balance 4 Hz
N° 04 · Bridges

Brass ribbons that keep arbors apart

Bridges clamp the upper pivots so no wheel can wander. A synthetic sapphire sits in each, because corundum wears slower than the steel it carries. These are cut open so the train shows through.

21 jewels · 4 bridges · steel screws
N° 05 · Dial

A dial with the middle taken out

A skeleton dial removes everything that is not structural. What is left: a flange, twelve numerals, a minute track, and a power-reserve sub-dial at two o'clock. On this page the reserve is spent by scrolling.

XII numerals · 60 minute ticks · 40 h reserve
N° 06 · Hands

The only honest output

Hour and minute hands ride friction-fitted on the centre arbor. The seconds hand sits on the fourth wheel's own pivot at six, so it steps eight times a second in time with the beat. They are reading your clock right now.

H 1/12 rph · M 1 rph · S 1 rpm

    Four meshes turn a slow spring into a fast beat

    IIThe beat

    One oscillation is two ticks

    The balance swings through centre twice per cycle, and every passage flips the fork. The brass trace is the balance angle, the blue trace the fork. Notice the fork only moves while the balance is near zero: a watch spends most of its life doing nothing, very precisely.
