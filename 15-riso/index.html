A print collective in Ketterby: four people and one temperamental duplicator, making zines, flyers and mistakes two colours at a time. Issue #7 is about what happens when one ink sits on another and neither gives way. It is late. It is also very pink.

INSIDE #7

Twenty-four pages, two inks, one staple that was not supposed to be there. We are letting you read eight of them. Press a line to open the zine at that page.

Contributors' note. Cover by Mina. Inside art also by Mina, mostly. The wobbly bits are Dez's thumb.

Pages 9 to 24 are for subscribers in the shop, on paper, because paper is the whole point.

READ IT

Pages 1 to 8 of 24, scanned off the actual copies. Arrow keys turn the page. Hover the book and the inks drift.

Cover

TWO PASSES

  1. 1

    Separate

    The artwork is split into one layer per ink. Each layer is plain black and white. The colour is a decision for later.

  2. 2

    Burn

    Brenda burns each layer into a thin master wrapped round a drum. No plates. A smell like warm crayons.

  3. 3

    Pass one

    Paper through for pink. Fast ink, slow dry. We wait. We eat toast. Do not touch the toast near the paper.

  4. 4

    Pass two

    Back through for blue, fed by hand and lined up by eye. The eye is wrong by about a millimetre. That millimetre is the whole point.

THE GANG

Dolores Pike

Editor, ink-fingers

Writes the letters, loses the receipts. Believes the em dash is a structural element and will not be argued with before 10am.

Dez Amadi

Drum whisperer

Can hear when a master is about to tear. Has opinions about glossy stock which are not repeatable in print.

Mina Hollis

Illustration

Draws everything in two layers, then argues with both of them. Responsible for the sun on the front of this site.

Brenda

Duplicator, 1998. Not a person.

Does about 130 sheets a minute when she feels like it. Jams on Tuesdays and on any sheet Dez describes as "fine". Rated for a lifetime of ten thousand masters; we are on about nine thousand, and we are not discussing it.
